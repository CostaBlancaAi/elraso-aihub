Chrome can fill in forms using your Gmail

Google’s Chrome browser can now search your Gmail for things like booking numbers, addresses and card numbers, then suggest them on websites. You choose whether to turn it on. For now it’s only in the USA.

What it means for you: handy, but it lets the browser read your email. If it arrives here, think before you switch it on, and never let anyone “set it up” for you over the phone.

(source: TechCrunch, 28 Oct 2026)

21 October 2026 · Spain

A Spanish political party posted a deepfake video

The PP put out a 51-second video that used AI to copy the faces and voices of six people linked to the PSOE, including the Prime Minister’s wife. They appear to “confess” to crimes. It said it was made with AI, but only in tiny, faint writing at the bottom. It reached almost two million views, and the PSOE has threatened legal action.

What it means for you: fake videos of real people are now everyday news, even in elections. Look for an “AI” label, and don’t share a shocking video until you’ve checked it somewhere you trust.

(source: The Olive Press, 22 Oct 2026)

Think differently: what’s real now
The short version

AI can now copy a voice, a face and a way of writing. So seeing and hearing isn’t proof any more.

That’s not a reason to panic. It’s a reason to think differently: slow down, check things your way, and keep up with what’s changing. This hub does the keeping up for you.

What we know now
  • AI is part of everyday life. About 900 million people use ChatGPT every week. (OpenAI, reported by eWEEK, 27 Feb 2026)
  • A voice can be copied from a few seconds of sound. Three seconds of audio gave an 85% match. (McAfee study, via Help Net Security, 8 May 2023)
  • One photo can become a talking video. Add a voice recording, and the face moves and speaks. (PetaPixel, 5 Feb 2025; Tom’s Guide, Apr 2024)
  • Almost nobody can spot every fake. In a test of 2,000 people, only 2 got them all right. And 39% of over-65s had never heard of deepfakes. (iProov, 12 Feb 2025)
  • It’s happening in Spain. Police warn of calls using a family member’s copied voice, asking for money in a hurry. People over 55 are the main target. (Qué!, 16 Apr 2026, in Spanish)
  • New EU rules have started. Since 2 August 2026, a chatbot must tell you it’s AI, and deepfakes must be labelled. From 2 December 2026, apps that make fake nude pictures of real people without consent are banned. (EU AI Act as amended by Regulation 2026/1744, via ActuIA)
What we don’t know yet
  • Whether labels will help much. Honest companies will label fakes. Scammers break the law already, so don’t wait for a label. (Our view)
  • What it means for jobs. In a poll of US economists, almost two in three said they were unsure whether AI will push up unemployment over the next ten years. (Chicago Booth Review, 3 Aug 2026)
  • How fast the rules will catch up. Some of the EU’s biggest AI rules have been pushed back to December 2027 and August 2028. (ActuIA)
  • The big long-term questions. 75 experts writing for governments said the future of AI is “remarkably uncertain”. Serious people disagree. (International AI safety report, via CTV News, 18 May 2024)
What might come next

These are possibilities, not predictions.

  • AI that does jobs for you. Some AI can already fill in forms and shop online for you. Expect more of this in everyday apps. (TechSpot, Jul 2025)
  • Live fakes on video calls. Researchers have shown a talking face made from one photo, in real time. (Microsoft VASA-1, via Tom’s Guide)
  • Real help, too. Used well, AI can translate a Spanish letter, explain a form or keep you company in a new hobby. We’ll show you how, safely. (Our view)

The golden rule

Not expecting it?

Don’t answer it.

If it’s real, they’ll find another way. If anyone asks for money, a code or a password, that’s the red flag. However real they sound. Even if they say they are your bank, the police or your family.

Then:

  1. 1
    Let it ring. Don’t reply or tap.Ignore the message or code. Picked up by mistake? Just hang up. You don’t have to be polite.
  2. 2
    Worried it’s real? Check it your way.Use the bank’s own app, the number on your bank card, or ring family on the number saved in your phone. Never the number that contacted you, or a number in the message.
  3. 3
    Not sure? Tell someone you trustbefore you do anything with money.

What is a “code”?

The short number your bank or WhatsApp texts you, like “Your code is 482915”. Only you type it, into an app you opened yourself. Nobody real will ever ask you for it.

Agree a family code word tonight

If “family” calls in a panic asking for money, ask for the word. AI can copy a voice. It can’t guess your word. Don’t write it down.

If something has gone wrong

Do these in order. Don’t be embarrassed. It happens to clever people every day.

1. Your bankBlock your card yourself in your bank’s app. Then ring the 24-hour number on the back of your card and ask them to stop the payment.
2. Message 017 on WhatsAppSpain’s free, private help line for scams and hacks. Easiest way: send a WhatsApp message to 900 116 117. Write in simple English, or use Google Translate. 8am to 11pm, every day.Open WhatsApp to 017Or ring 017 (in Spanish).
3. Report it: Guardia Civil 062El Raso is covered by the Guardia Civil. Ring them to report a scam. Keep screenshots of the messages first.Call 062

112 is for emergencies only

Ring 112 if someone is in danger right now: a fire, an accident, someone ill, or a stranger at your door who won’t leave. It answers in English.

Not for a scam text, a hacked account or lost money. For those, use your bank, 017 and 062 above.

What is 017, and what happens when I message them?
  • Who they are. 017 is Spain’s national cyber help line, run by INCIBE, a government agency. It’s free and private. (INCIBE FAQ)
  • What they do. Experts tell you what to do next: how to secure your accounts, what to do if you’ve been tricked, and who to report it to. They give technical, legal and emotional help. (INCIBE FAQ)
  • What they don’t do. They’re not the police, so you can’t report a crime to them. They can’t get into your phone or fix it for you. (La Moncloa, 14 Jul 2026, in Spanish)
  • How to reach them. WhatsApp 900 116 117, Telegram @INCIBE017, or phone 017. Every day, 8am to 11pm. (La Moncloa)
  • What happens next. First they ask what it’s about: you, a child, or a business. Then answer their questions as best you can. You can’t send photos or files in the chat. (INCIBE FAQ)
  • Language. It’s a Spanish service, and INCIBE doesn’t say which other languages it offers. Message in simple English or with Google Translate. If they reply in Spanish, translate their reply the same way. Better still, do it with your helper. (Our tip)
  • Richie is checking this. I’m testing 017 myself, in English, to see how useful it is for us. I’ll share what I find on this page and the WhatsApp channel.

“Black October”: what it means for you

This month, people using AI found weaknesses in software that runs millions of websites. They’re being fixed, but some customer details were taken first.

What it means for you: expect more scam calls, texts and emails for a while. Some may know your name or your address. That doesn’t make them real.

You can’t fix the websites. You can lock your own doors. Start with the list below.

What to do now: your to-do list

Best way: do it together

Pick one or two people you trust, or a small group of friends. Meet for a coffee, bring your phones and this page, and do the list together. One person reads the step out, the other taps.

A grandchild on a video call works too. Nobody has to remember it all on their own.

Or see the whole list here. At home, on your home Wi-Fi. One at a time.

  • Update your phone. Go to Settings, then Software Update (iPhone) or System Update (Android). Never from a pop-up or a text.
  • Lock WhatsApp. Settings, then Account, then Two-step verification, then Turn on. Then check Linked devices and log out anything you don’t know.
  • Email and bank first. Give each its own password. Then turn on two-step login for both.
  • Check for leaks. Type haveibeenpwned.com into your browser and enter your email. Change any password it flags.
  • Turn on payment alerts in your banking app.
  • Cut sales calls. Join the free Lista Robinson at listarobinson.es. It can take up to two months to work.
  • Agree your family code word.

Worth knowing

A password manager remembers every password for you. Your phone already has one built in. Proton Pass has a good free version. I earn nothing from recommending it.

A handy clue: since 17 October, big companies in Spain must make sales calls from numbers starting 400. A “company” selling from an ordinary mobile? Be careful.

Don’t buy anything in a panic.

Calls, messages and scams

I clicked a link. What now?

Didn’t type anything in? Close it. That’s usually it.

Typed in a password or card? Block your card in your bank’s app, change that password, then message 017.

Can AI really copy my voice?

Yes. From a short video or a voice note.

That’s why your family code word matters. AI can’t guess it.

Can scammers fake a notification?

Not inside your real apps. But texts, websites and calendar invites can look like one.

Don’t tap it. Open the app yourself.

Is it safe to scan QR codes?

Usually. Check the web address it shows before you tap.

Watch out for stickers on parking meters and posters: fake codes get stuck over real ones.

My friend’s WhatsApp was hacked. What do I do?

Phone them. A real call, not a message.

Don’t tap anything “they” send, and never send money or codes.

Someone rang from “Microsoft” about my computer.

Hang up. Microsoft, Amazon and your internet company never ring out of the blue.

Never let anyone onto your computer because of a call.

A delivery driver is ringing me. Do I answer?

Expecting a parcel? Yes, answer it. A real driver only asks where to leave it.

Asked for money, a code or to tap a link? Hang up. AI can fake the voice. It can’t change what they’re after.

The “bank” rang, and now someone’s coming to collect my card.

Never hand it over. Banks never send anyone to collect cards.

Shut the door, ring the number on the back of your card, then ring the Guardia Civil on 062.

An email says they hacked my camera, and it shows my old password.

It’s a bluff. Don’t pay, don’t reply.

They got the old password from a leak. Change it anywhere you still use it.

Your phone, bank and accounts

Is my bank account safe?

Banks weren’t the weak lock this month. The bigger risk is being talked into a payment.

Turn on payment alerts in your banking app, and keep the golden rule.

How do I know if I’ve been hacked?

Messages you didn’t send. Unknown linked devices. Password emails you didn’t ask for.

Not sure? Message 017 on WhatsApp, 900 116 117. It’s free (in Spanish).

Should I stop banking online?

No. The bank’s own app, with two-step login, is a good way to bank.

Just never act on a call or a message. Open the app yourself.

Should I buy antivirus or a data removal service?

Not in a panic. Do the free things first.

Updates, the golden rule, two-step login. Most of the protection is free.

Is the free Wi-Fi here safe?

Fine for browsing and WhatsApp.

For banking, use mobile data or your home Wi-Fi.

A robber makes me unlock my phone with my face. Isn’t that dangerous?

Hand it over. You matter more than a phone.

Then switch on theft protection tonight: it stops a thief taking over your accounts. It’s on the take-home checklist.

My phone suddenly says “No service” at home.

Ring your phone company straight away, from another phone.

Someone may have copied your SIM to get your bank codes. Then ring your bank.

WhatsApp, Facebook and listening devices

Is WhatsApp safe?

Your chats are locked so only you and the other person can read them. WhatsApp says not even it or Meta can.

The real risks are scam messages and someone getting your 6-digit code. Turn on your WhatsApp PIN.

If I follow your WhatsApp channel, can you see my number?

No. Following doesn’t show your number to me or to anyone else.

If you’re already saved in my phone, WhatsApp tells me you follow. That’s all.

Do I have to reply to the channel?

You can’t. It’s one-way: I post, you read, when it suits you.

You can tap an emoji or answer a poll if you like. Nobody can message you from it.

Is Facebook safe to use?

Fine for keeping in touch, with a few settings.

Set your posts to Friends only. Skip quizzes, “you’ve won” posts and friend requests from people you’re already friends with.

Can my TV or smart speaker hear me?

They can. A big investigation in September says some LG TVs recorded sound even when they looked off. LG denies it.

Don’t use voice control? Turn it off in Settings, plus “viewing information”. Away for a while? Switch off at the wall.

Is my phone listening to me?

Not in the way people fear.

The real risks today are scam messages and shared codes.

Verwacht u het niet? Neem niet op. Is het echt, dan vinden ze wel een andere manier. Vraagt iemand om geld, een code of een wachtwoord? Dat is het alarmsignaal. Wie het ook vraagt. Hoe echt het ook klinkt. Ook als ze zeggen dat ze van uw bank, de politie of uw familie zijn.

Daarna:

  1. Laat het overgaan. Antwoord niet en tik nergens op. Per ongeluk opgenomen? Hang gewoon op.

Español: la regla de oro

¿No lo esperaba? No conteste. Si es de verdad, encontrarán otra manera. Si le piden dinero, un código o una contraseña, esa es la señal de alarma. Lo pida quien lo pida. Por muy real que parezca. Aunque digan que son su banco, la policía o su familia.

Después:

  1. Déjelo sonar. No responda ni toque nada. ¿Lo cogió sin querer? Cuelgue sin más.

¿Ya ha perdido dinero? Bloquee primero usted mismo la tarjeta en la app de su banco y llame al número de 24 horas de la tarjeta. Luego al 017, la línea gratuita de ayuda (de 8 a 23 h, WhatsApp 900 116 117). Denuncia: Guardia Civil 062. Emergencias: 112.
