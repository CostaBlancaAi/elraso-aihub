The golden rule

Not expecting it?

Don’t answer it.

Then ring a number you already know. If anyone asks for money, a code or a password, that’s the red flag. However real they sound. Even if they say they are your bank, the police or your family.

Then:

  1. 1
    Don’t answer. Don’t reply.Let it ring. Ignore the message or code. Picked up by mistake? Just hang up. You don’t have to be polite.
  2. 2
    Ring a number you already know.Like the one on your bank card, or on your fridge card from the workshop. Never the number that just called you, or a number in the message.
  3. 3
    Tell someone you trustbefore you do anything with money.

What is a “code”?

The short number your bank or WhatsApp texts you, like “Your code is 482915”. Only you type it, into an app you opened yourself. Nobody real will ever ask you for it.

Agree a family code word tonight

If “family” calls in a panic asking for money, ask for the word. AI can copy a voice. It can’t guess your word. Don’t write it down.

If something has gone wrong

Do these in order. Don’t be embarrassed. It happens to clever people every day.

What’s happening

24 October 2026

“Black October”: what it means for you

This month, people using AI found weaknesses in software that runs millions of websites. They’re being fixed, but some customer details were taken first.

What it means for you: expect more scam calls, texts and emails for a while. Some may know your name or your address. That doesn’t make them real.

You can’t fix the websites. You can lock your own doors. Start with the list below.

Do this tonight

At home, on your home Wi-Fi. One at a time. Tick them off as you go.

  • Update your phone. Go to Settings, then Software Update (iPhone) or System Update (Android). Never from a pop-up or a text.
  • Lock WhatsApp. Settings, then Account, then Two-step verification, then Turn on. Then check Linked devices and log out anything you don’t know.
  • Email and bank first. Give each its own password. Then turn on two-step login for both.
  • Check for leaks. Type haveibeenpwned.com into your browser and enter your email. Change any password it flags.
  • Turn on payment alerts in your banking app.
  • Cut sales calls. Join the free Lista Robinson at listarobinson.es. It can take up to two months to work.
  • Agree your family code word.

Worth knowing

A password manager remembers every password for you. Your phone already has one built in. Proton Pass has a good free version. I earn nothing from recommending it.

A handy clue: since 17 October, big companies in Spain must make sales calls from numbers starting 400. A “company” selling from an ordinary mobile? Be careful.

Don’t buy anything in a panic.

Nederlands: de gouden regel

Verwacht u het niet? Neem dan niet op. Bel daarna een nummer dat u al kent. Vraagt iemand om geld, een code of een wachtwoord? Dat is het alarmsignaal. Wie het ook vraagt. Hoe echt het ook klinkt. Ook als ze zeggen dat ze van uw bank, de politie of uw familie zijn.

Daarna:

  1. Neem niet op. Antwoord niet. Laat het overgaan. Per ongeluk opgenomen? Hang gewoon op.
  2. Bel een nummer dat u al kent, zoals het nummer op uw bankpas. Gebruik nooit een nummer uit het gesprek of bericht.
  3. Vertel het iemand die u vertrouwt voordat u iets met geld doet.

Al geld kwijt? Bel eerst uw bank, dan 017, de gratis hulplijn (in het Spaans) (dagelijks 8–23 uur, WhatsApp 900 116 117).

De workshops zijn in het Engels. Neem gerust iemand mee die kan vertalen.

Español: la regla de oro

¿No lo esperaba? No conteste. Después llame a un número que ya conozca. Si le piden dinero, un código o una contraseña, esa es la señal de alarma. Lo pida quien lo pida. Por muy real que parezca. Aunque digan que son su banco, la policía o su familia.

Después:

  1. No conteste. No responda. Déjelo sonar. ¿Lo cogió sin querer? Cuelgue sin más.
  2. Llame a un número que ya conozca, como el de su tarjeta del banco. Nunca use un número de la llamada o del mensaje.
  3. Cuénteselo a alguien de confianza antes de hacer nada con dinero.

¿Ya ha perdido dinero? Llame primero a su banco y luego al 017, la línea gratuita de ayuda (de 8 a 23 h, WhatsApp 900 116 117).

Los talleres son en inglés. Venga con alguien que le pueda traducir.
